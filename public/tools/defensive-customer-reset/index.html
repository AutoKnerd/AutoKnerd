The Defensive Customer Reset

The customer walks in guarded, arms crossed, answering in one word. A four-step reset your consultants can use on the floor, and a 10-minute drill to practice it before the real thing.

Instant win: In your next meeting, have one consultant play a guarded customer for 60 seconds while a teammate responds live with no coaching.

Why this matters

Every consultant on your floor has met this customer.

Defensive, arms crossed, short answers, already deciding whether to trust you or leave., most consultants do one of three things.

Freeze

They go quiet, wait for the customer to open up, and lose the first minute they needed.

Push

They talk faster, pitch harder and confirm every fear the customer walked in with.

Write them off

They decide the deal is dead before they say hello.

The guard is learned. Customers sit in the car with their hands on the wheel rehearsing "don't say too much, don't get trapped, stay sharp." By the time they reach you, they feel they have already lost. The wall is self-defense. You have not swung a punch.

The reset: four steps

Lower the guard first. Sell second.

1. Read it as safety
Goal: stop taking it personally

Think"They don't trust me yet. That is information, not an insult." Short answers and crossed arms mean the customer has not decided it is safe.

2. Take the pressure off
Goal: remove the thing they are bracing against

Say"Take your time. You're not buying today, and that's okay. It just gives me more time to help you get the right information." Honor the distance they asked for.

3. Change the subject to them
Goal: one real question about their life

Ask"What's important to you? What are you driving now?" Or something light that has nothing to do with the sale, like "What do you take in your coffee?" Make it a conversation, not a questionnaire.

4. Partner, do not control
Goal: work on it together

Say"Let's figure out the right one together. You tell me what matters, and I'll tell you what I see."

What not to do

The managers' phrase to retire.

Skip this

"Go out there and control your customer."

Trying to control another human is a main reason customers put up defenses in the first place.
Say this

"Build a partnership. Work with them to find the right vehicle."

Control, in the useful sense, means setting up a relationship where you both work toward the same outcome.

The 10-minute practice drill

Practice the hard customer hundreds of times before the real one. Start with a few.

The customer

Plays guarded: arms crossed, one-word answers, "just looking." Later, switch to high-anxiety, then super happy.

The consultant

Responds in real time. No pauses, no coaching from the sideline.

The observer

Stays silent and marks one thing: the exact moment the customer was lost, or the moment they opened up.

The point: you cannot coach what you cannot see. After each rep, the observer names where the customer was lost, and the consultant tries that moment again.

Manager rollout: 15 minutes

Run it in this week's sales meeting.

  • Minutes 0 to 3. Ask the room: who has frozen, pushed or written off a guarded customer this week? Hands up. Read the four steps.
  • Minutes 3 to 5. Split into trios: customer, consultant, observer.
  • Minutes 5 to 12. Two-minute reps, three rotations so everyone plays every role. Observers name the moment the customer was lost.
  • Minutes 12 to 15. Each consultant picks the one step they will use on their very next guarded customer and tells a partner. Schedule next week's drill.

Inspect the reps and where they broke, not just the deals.
