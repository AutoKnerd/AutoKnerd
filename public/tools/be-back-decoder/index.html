The Be-Back Decoder

Six things customers say right before they disappear, what each one really means, and what to say instead so they stay in the conversation.

Instant win: Ask each consultant to write down three exit lines they heard this week, then reframe one out loud before the next up.

Why this matters

The idea to start from.

Lies are protection

They are rarely aggression. A nervous customer is finding a way to reason with you.

Distrust is the problem

Customers who said "I need to talk about it" later admitted they did not trust the numbers. The line was not the issue. The missing trust was.

Call in, not out

When you catch the line, do not call them on it. Reframe it to lower their fear and restore safety to the conversation.

"Buyers are liars" is a copout. Ask a poor question like "what do you want to pay a month?", hear 250, show 500, and call the customer a liar, and you caused that. Retire the saying.

The decoder

When you hear it, think translation. Then say the right-hand side.

"I'm just looking."

What it often meansI don't feel comfortable. I don't trust that I can explore freely.

Say instead"Totally cool to browse. Walk around as much as you like. I know this inventory well, so let me know if I can support that in any way." Then start a real conversation: "What's important to you? Why are you driving that one?"

"I need to talk to my spouse."

What it often meansAn escape hatch. I feel pressured and I need distance.

Say instead"I understand if this feels like pressure. It's a car dealership and pressure shows up in different ways. Let's take a quick look at the car to make sure it's the right one, so nothing falls apart when you talk it over at home. Would you like a full breakdown of the vehicle to take with you?"

"I'm not buying today."

What it often meansSelf-defense. The wall is up and you have not even swung a punch.

Say instead"That's okay. It gives me more time to educate you on the product. What do you take in your coffee?" Move them out of the defensive zone and into a conversation.

"I can get a better deal elsewhere."

What it often meansA power move, or trust that never formed. Often the customer is pushing on the deal because we could not stop talking about it.

Say instead"You may be able to. Let's talk about what matters to you and make sure the match is just right." Then back up: how did I open this and where did trust slip?

"I already know everything about this car."

What it often meansThe ego shield: don't make me feel dumb.

Say instead"Great, teach me. What are you hearing online? What color do you like?" Customers love telling you what they have learned. Play off their research.

"We'll be back."

What it often meansOne of the five above wearing a polite coat. Ask yourself which one, and where the pressure or distrust started.

Say instead"Of course. Before you go, what's the one thing you'd want to feel sure about? I'd hate for it to fall apart at home." Then listen to the answer.

Where the "better deal" line comes from

The 15% rule.

Skip this

Talk about the deal, the payment and the discount until the customer feels they have to fight for their business.

Then you run to the manager to discount more, and nobody wins.
Do this

Commit to 15% of the conversation being about the car. The rest is about their life.

Motivation gets people to buy. Pressure pushes them away.

Manager rollout: 15 minutes

Run it in this week's sales meeting.

  • Minutes 0 to 3. Ask the room to call out exit lines they heard this week. Write them on the board. Point out that most match the six above.
  • Minutes 3 to 6. Each consultant writes three of those lines on paper, plus how they felt and what they said back.
  • Minutes 6 to 11. Pairs role-play. One says the exit line. The other answers with a reframe: acknowledge, lower the pressure, ask one real question. Swap.
  • Minutes 11 to 15. Every consultant picks one customer from the last week who said "we'll be back" and sends a short, no-pressure message today. Retire "buyers are liars" from the floor.

Weekly inspection

Inspect what we said back, not just who came back.
