Post-Discovery Handoff

A one-page card that carries what the customer told you in discovery onto the car, so every feature you show answers "show me that you heard me."

Instant win:On your next walkaround, tie every feature to something the customer said, starting with "You told me..." If you cannot, skip the feature.

Why this matters

The moment right after discovery is where customers get lost.

365points of walkaround dropped on a couple fresh from a product training. They left and said the car had too much equipment
5 to 7reasons is all a customer needs to buy, so sell those reasons and nothing else

The customer is thinking one thing: show me that you heard me. Specs without context tell them you were not listening. A walking brochure is available free on the manufacturer's website. Be the proof they came to the right place.

Spec robot versus trusted guide

Same feature. Very different walkaround.

Spec robot

"And we've got a panoramic sunroof right here."

Then straight on to the next feature, hoping they work out how it changes their life.
Trusted guide

"You said your kids love road trips and you love taking them. Check out the panoramic sunroof. At night the kids can look up at the stars, it keeps the ride feeling open, and it means a little less fighting in the back."

The feature is tied to something they said.

The Feature, Benefit, Why card

Three columns. If the Why column is blank, the feature does not belong in the presentation.

FeatureBenefit (plain words)Why (what the customer said)
Adaptive cruiseLess driver fatigue on long drives"You commute an hour each way."
Blind spot monitorWarns you when a car is hiding in your blind spot"Your daughter is about to start driving."
Wireless phone connectionNo cables, quick connect, everything on the screen when you get in"Tech makes your life easier when you're rushing from meeting to meeting."
Fully flat seatsFits the stroller and the cooler"Weekend soccer trips are your family's escape."
Hands-free liftgateMini story: you're carrying groceries and the gate opens without you dropping anything"You said you dropped a lot last winter."

Notice the safety example: the hot button is "safety," but the cord with emotion on it is "my daughter starts driving soon." Show the thing that prevents the accident, not just the airbags.

Three rules for the card

Run every feature through them before it leaves your mouth.

No Why, no show

If you cannot check off the Why, take the feature out. It clogs the presentation and the test drive.

Write a mini story

Do not write a bland benefit. Even three words of story beat a spec: "groceries in hand, gate opens."

Use the Why as your closer

End each feature with "That's why I showed you this." Each yes is a small close, so you never need a closing class.

The self-check

Ask it silently before every feature.

Ask yourself

"Am I proving I listened to what they said, or proving I studied the brochure?"

Product knowledge is not the royalty skill. Working with people is.
What to expect when you do it

Shorter negotiations, lighter objections, and trust that builds faster.

There is less to object to when you only showed what they wanted.

Manager rollout: 15 minutes

Run it in this week's sales meeting. Print the card or have people sketch the three columns.

  • Minutes 0 to 3. Tell the 365-point story. Ask: when did you last show a feature the customer never mentioned?
  • Minutes 3 to 6. Everyone picks an imaginary customer and three features, then fills in Feature, Benefit and Why.
  • Minutes 6 to 13. Four chairs in the meeting room stand in for a car. Each person gives a three-minute walkaround using the card, tying each feature back. Teammates watch for a Why that was not the customer's own words.
  • Minutes 13 to 15. Swap cards with a teammate. Everyone commits to using a card on their next customer.

Weekly inspection

Inspect the card and the walkaround, not the closing rate.
