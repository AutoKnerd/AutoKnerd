Ego Check

A one-page tool to spot ego before it costs you a deal, with a 15-minute rollout you can run in your next sales meeting.

Instant win:Ask every consultant this one question before their next customer: "Am I trying to help this person make a great decision, or am I trying to prove something?"

Why this matters

Two consultants, same product knowledge, different results.

Mike

Sharp, smooth, knows the product cold. When a customer pushes on price, features or "does it come in blue?", his shoulders stiffen, his smile turns fake and his voice drops.

He could close, but he could not close at gross. He was usually right. His ego was louder than his empathy.
Jamie

Doesn't brag, doesn't rush, listens more than she talks. She partners with the customer instead of fighting them.

She closes at the same rate as Mike, and the customers feel good about working with a great person, not just about a deal.

Ego is not confidence. Ego is fear in fancy clothes: fear of being wrong, losing control or looking weak. It does not protect you. It exposes you.

Five signals ego is driving

Catch it in the moment. Any one of these means pause, breathe, reset.

  • You feel the urge to talk over someone.
  • You are prepping your comeback while they are still talking.
  • You need to win the conversation.
  • You are judging their intelligence, lifestyle or budget in the back of your head.
  • You feel insulted when they say no.

Ego disguises and the swap

It is not always chest-puffing. Most of the time it is subtle.

Ego looks likeSay this instead
Correcting the customer ("I saw a model online with different wheels")"It comes in a lot of different packages. Let me pull those up and figure out exactly what you're looking at."
Taking offense at "I'm just looking""Perfect. That gives me more time to help you explore options and find the right vehicle with no pressure."
Downplaying an objection to dodge rejectionStay curious. Ask about it instead of arguing with it.
Rushing the close because you know where this is goingLet the customer do every step they need to do.
Re-interpreting their story to educate themLet them finish. Reflect back what they said before you add anything.
Bragging about units soldTalk about their life, not your year-to-date.
Hearing "I need to think about it" as personalTake it as information and keep the door open without getting defensive.

Managers: your ego sets the store's tone

Ego is not just personal. It is culture.

Listen in meetings

If meetings are only you talking, your team learns to posture instead of listen.

Reward vulnerability

If you punish it, they hide weakness behind fake confidence and you never learn who your best people are.

Celebrate how, not just that

Praise how the sale was made, not only that it was made. Model humility and reward empathy.

Manager rollout: 15 minutes

Run it in this week's sales meeting.

  • Minutes 0 to 3. Tell the Mike and Jamie story. Ask: which one do customers buy from twice? Then ask each person to name their own most common ego disguise from the table.
  • Minutes 3 to 6. Read the five signals aloud. Everyone circles the one they feel most often. You go first and own yours.
  • Minutes 6 to 12. Pairs role-play a customer who corrects them, says "I'm just looking" or says "I need to think about it." The consultant uses the swap line. The partner scores whether they stayed curious.
  • Minutes 12 to 15. Everyone writes the one question on a sticky note for their desk: "Am I helping right now, or am I protecting my own image?" Set the day you will check the scorecard below.

Weekly inspection

Inspect the behavior, not the result. Tally from a manager walk-around or a quick end-of-day huddle.
