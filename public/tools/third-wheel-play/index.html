The Third Wheel Play

The friend, parent or spouse who has not said a word until the numbers hit the table. A five-move play that turns the one who derails the deal into the one who closes it.

Instant win: On the next two-person visit, ask both of them "What's most important to the both of you?" and write down what the quiet one says.

Why this matters

The numbers.

61%of buyers say friends and family were their biggest influence
83%trust word of mouth from family and friends
5 to 10%how far family opinions still sway people, even over reliability and budget

The third wheel is not a distraction. They are part of the decision. If they dislike you, you hear about it at the worst moment. If they like you, the customer trusts you more. A trusted spouse or best friend can outweigh even 500 five-star reviews.

Why they speak up at the end

Three forces.

Fitting in

Buyers do not want to look foolish in front of people they care about.

Leaning on someone

Specs, financing and rates feel uncertain, so people lean on whoever is next to them, even a non-expert.

Trust multiplies

The more a customer trusts the person in the room, the more weight their words carry.

The play

Five moves, in order. Do not fight the third wheel. Ride with them.

1. Greet both
Goal: acknowledge they exist

DoSay hello to both at the door. A lot of third wheels sit in the back wondering whether anyone knows they are there. Gently find out who they are to the buyer, without getting too personal.

2. Open the table
Goal: ask both, not just the buyer

Say"What's most important to the both of you?" Or if they have researched together: "It sounds like you've done your research together. Walk me through your top priorities, both of you."

3. Write it down
Goal: let them talk, even if grumpy

DoIf the third wheel is aggravated, write down exactly what they say. They are telling you what to do. Never scream back. Give them a few minutes to vent, and learn why they came. It may be pure moral support.

4. Acknowledge, validate, redirect
Goal: bring their priorities into the plan

Say"So you want to avoid [their concern], and we need to land around [their stated target]. Do you agree?" Validate their opinion, make them feel important, then redirect back to the stated priorities.

5. Ask them to weigh in
Goal: the third wheel becomes the closer

Say"Our numbers match what you worked out. What do you think, is this a good one for your friend?" Let the one who checked the math confirm it. Keep it honest and warm, never a trap.

One line to keep, one to drop

85% of the conversation should be about the people in front of you and 15% about the product.

Skip this

Talk only to the buyer and ignore the other person until they speak.

Ignoring the influencer means longer negotiations and more lost deals.
Say this

"What's most important to the both of you?"

It opens the table, makes the third wheel part of the process and tells you what you need to know.

Spot the type

Types of third wheel, and how to work each one.

Manager rollout: 15 minutes

Run it in this week's sales meeting.

  • Minutes 0 to 3. Read the 61% and 83% numbers. Ask: when did a quiet tag-along last wreck a deal at the table?
  • Minutes 3 to 6. Walk the five moves and the four types.
  • Minutes 6 to 12. Trios role-play: buyer, third wheel, consultant. Make the third wheel grumpy or quiet. Consultants run moves 1 to 4. Do not be jerks about it, and aim for everyone laughing by the end.
  • Minutes 12 to 15. Each consultant names the next customer pair they expect and which move they will open with. Set a day to review the notes.

Weekly inspection

Inspect whether the second person was included, not just whether the deal closed.
