Discovery Before The Test Drive

A one-page guide to earning the customer's story before you ever grab a key, so the drive becomes a conversation and not a silent loop around the block.

Instant win:Before your next test drive, ask permission to ask questions, ask one of the three openers below, and say their answer back to them before you pull the car around.

Why this matters

The numbers.

85%of the conversation should be about the customer, only 15% about the product
45 minis about as long as a customer wants to be there, and two hours is the most you want to spend on one customer

The story behind it: a customer said yes to trunk size, yes to the kids, yes to the dog. Five minutes later they were on a drive that felt awkward, and they came back with "thanks, we'll just think about it." The consultant worked the vehicle and never worked the person. If a customer has not told you their story, they do not yet trust you enough to ride along.

The reframe

Discovery is not qualifying. It is aligning.

Guide, not cop

You are not there to filter out weak leads or interrogate. You are there to find the signal in their story and build on it.

Slow is fast

If discovery feels rushed to you, it is meaningless to them. Slow down and listen harder.

Customer is the hero

The car is not the hero of the deal. The customer is. You are the guide.

The four moves

Run them in order before the test drive.

1. Ask permission
Goal: stop sounding like an officer

Say"I totally get it. I'm not here to pressure you. It doesn't help me and it doesn't help you. I just want to make your time as useful as possible. Mind if I ask a few questions so I don't waste any of it?"

2. Ask a low-pressure opener
Goal: get the story rolling

Pick one"What changed recently that brought you in?"  /  "What does your current car not do well for you?"  /  "What needs to be different this time around?"

3. Read the invisible signals
Goal: match the customer's pace

Watch forAre they rushed? Anxious? Keeping a spouse or kid calm? If a question makes them squirm, back up to a softer one. Your step one may be their step three.

4. Mirror and validate
Goal: earn a yes by proving you listened

SayRepeat what they said in your own voice, then confirm it: "So the last two stores never pulled around the right color, and you want this one to be different. Did I get that right?" That "yes" is a micro commitment, and it is where trust starts.

Three customers, three openers

Same goal each time: give them a safety net, not pressure.

The high-energy shopper

"It looks like you've clearly done your homework. Mind if I ask you a few things so we can make the most of your time today?"

A big fluffy stop sign. It slows them down without killing momentum.
The skeptical buyer

"Totally fair. If I were you, I'd be cautious too. Want a little space for a moment, or would it help if I asked a couple of questions to narrow things down? I have a lot of cars here and they're all different."

Gives them room to think instead of crowbarring answers out of them.
The "I'm just looking" customer

Use humor or a reframe, then borrow one of the openers above.

They are usually curious, almost ready, or secretly hoping the right one jumps out. Every word matters here.

Skip this, do this

The warm-up trap versus real discovery.

Skip this

Smile, shake hands, "What brings you in today?", then a feature dump, a walk through the inventory and a test drive on the most expensive thing they point at.

Needs assessment used as a checklist. You hope for the best.
Do this

Ask permission, ask a low-pressure opener, read their signals, mirror their words, and only then choose the car and the drive.

You know who they are, so the drive extends the conversation.

Manager rollout: 15 minutes

Run it in this week's sales meeting.

  • Minutes 0 to 3. Tell the story above. Ask the room: how many of your last five test drives started with a real conversation about the customer's life?
  • Minutes 3 to 5. Read the four moves aloud. Each consultant circles the opener that sounds most like them.
  • Minutes 5 to 12. Pairs role-play the three customers. One plays the customer and acts rushed, skeptical, or "just looking." The partner runs moves 1 to 4 and finishes by mirroring back. Swap.
  • Minutes 12 to 15. Every consultant commits to run all four moves on their next up. Set the day you will ask each person what they learned.

Weekly inspection

Inspect the habit, not the result. Closing rate is the lagging number.
