The Lead Rescue Playbook

A one-page system for answering internet leads like a human, fast, and pulling the quiet ones back before they sit in someone else's car.

Instant win: Pull every internet lead from the last 24 hours and send a real human text to any without a reply, before lunch.

Why this matters

Three numbers that matter.

10 minhow fast customers expect a reply, some even faster
42 hrsaverage dealership wait for an answer
391%better conversion when a human replies within about the first minute

In those 42 hours the customer test drives somewhere else, a cousin names another brand, or they forget the form at all. Meanwhile another store is already in their inbox. Your lead is not cold. It was left alone.

Three rules for every lead reply

If a reply breaks one of these, rewrite it.

Human

A real person writes it. "Somebody will be reaching out to you shortly" is not a reply. And no AI-written replies; customers can tell.

Layered

Text to spark interest, call to let them hear your voice, email to hold the details. Three channels, one conversation.

Pressure-free

Pressure is the sledgehammer that raises anxiety. You are opening the door and telling them it is safe to come in.

The rescue sequence

Starts the moment the lead lands. Do not push it to the next business day.

First minutes
Goal: spark interest by text

Text"Hi, my name is [Name]. Saw you were checking out that [vehicle]. If you're anything like me, you've got 27 million tabs open trying to figure out which car to get. If you wouldn't mind, I'd like to help you close one of them."

Right after
Goal: create connection by phone

CallLet them hear your voice and your cadence. They are checking that you are someone they can trust and work with. Answer the question they actually asked.

Same day
Goal: cement the value by email

EmailSend the answers, details and anything else you want them to keep. Email supports the text and call. It does not replace them.

If you missed it
Goal: the redemption round

Text"Hey [Name], I dropped the ball and I am sorry. Your question came in and I had some good answers for it. Here they are. I'm still here and I promise it'll be worth your time."

Redemption: do and do not

A late reply is not a lost lead. A late reply with pressure is.

Skip this

Say nothing and tell yourself you'll deal with it in the morning. Or follow up with something lame and push for the appointment.

Silence is how the lead ends up in someone else's driveway. Pressure raises anxiety.
Say this

A short, humble apology, the answer to their question, and a promise that you are still here.

Humility and service build street credit with the customer. You are offering help, not applying pressure.

The graveyard rule

Your CRM keeps resurfacing old leads for a reason.

Some CRMs keep announcing that a lead from years ago is hot again., that is the system telling you now is a good time to reach out. Managers, open the CRM and look at what your consultants see. Check the settings and get the old leads moving, then humanize them. Treat each one like a person who filled out a form, not a row to scroll past.

Manager rollout: 15 minutes

Run it in this week's sales meeting.

  • Minutes 0 to 3. Read the three numbers above. Ask the room: how long did our last five internet leads wait for a human reply?
  • Minutes 3 to 6. Each consultant opens one real lead from this week and writes the first text in their own words, using the example as a guide.
  • Minutes 6 to 11. Pairs read their text out loud. The partner checks it against the three rules: human, layered, pressure-free. Anyone with a missed lead writes the redemption text.
  • Minutes 11 to 15. Send the texts from the room. Set a time today to check the lead log and the CRM graveyard together.

Weekly inspection

Inspect the behavior, not the result. Appointments are the lagging number.
