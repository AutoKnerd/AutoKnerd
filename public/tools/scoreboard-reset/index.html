Scoreboard Reset

A one-page guide for managers to stop the leaderboard from becoming a verdict on people, find the growers it overlooks, and coach on effort and service instead of just units.

Instant win:Today, replace "how many you got?" with "who did you help today?", and ask each consultant: "Did you serve well today?"

Why this matters

Three lines to remember.

Your number is not your nameIt is a metric and a milestone, not your identity.
Commission is a byproductIt is not a verdict on who you are.
Sales is serviceWhen it becomes survival, people shortcut the experience and customers feel it.

Say last month's unit count out loud. It is just a number. The leaderboard is a silent judge. Every name and every gap above you can start to feel like a reflection of you. The spiral looks like this: less connection, fewer sales, more pressure, and people do not want to be around you.

Closers versus growers

The board rewards one and ignores the other. Managers should look at both.

What the board rewards

Volume. The consultant moving the most metal.

Fast-moving deals often give up gross, because the customer makes up the value in negotiation.
What the board ignores

The grower, the person who sells fewer cars with a stronger experience and often better gross.

If someone drops well below their usual volume, get with them: "What are you doing?" Then run the averages of gross by volume tier and see who really produces.

Track units desk-side, because you need to know who is producing. But it should not be for the whole world to see. If your board is on the showroom floor, be more sensitive to your people.

Four costs of making the number your identity

If you see these in a consultant, it is time for a reset conversation.

Burnout

Same desk, same fast-food lunch, no energy. The cure is a reset: go do something fun.

Desperation

Customers pick up anxious energy. People do not want to be around it.

Toxic competition

The loud one running down everyone else. If they add nothing positive, give them distance.

Emotional detachment

Caring about the number more than the customer: pushing deals that do not make sense, fake smiles, shortcut experiences.

After a bad month: the reset

Reflect, adjust, keep going. It is not the customer.

1. Name it
Goal: separate the month from the person

SayNot "I had a bad month, therefore I am bad," or "I didn't sell today, therefore I'm lazy." A bad month is a bad month. Learn from it and come back stronger.

2. Ask what you are doing
Goal: find the behavior

Ask"If it did not work with the last three customers, what am I doing that I should stop?" The customer is not the problem, because a couple of months ago the same customers were saying yes.

3. Set an effort goal
Goal: a target you control

SayInstead of "I'll sell three today": "I'll follow up with 10 people and give five unforgettable demo drives." Call people who may just be waiting for someone to say hi.

4. Reflect daily
Goal: measure service

Ask"Did I serve well today?" And try a new metric, compassion per interaction: did you slow down and listen, and explain the numbers with honesty instead of rushing to the close?

The legacy question

Use it in a one-on-one.

Skip this

"He crushed it every month."

All it says is how many cars you sold.
Aim for this

"He made everybody on the floor better." "Customers trusted her with their kids." "She showed up even when she had nothing to gain."

Imagine your retirement party. What do you want them to say?

Other grounding tools: write down five things that make you proud outside of sales and keep them close, or say it aloud: "I am not my commission. I am my character."

Manager rollout: 15 minutes

Run it in this week's sales meeting. Lead by going first.

  • Minutes 0 to 3. Ask everyone to say last month's unit count out loud. Then read: "Your number is not your name." Share one slow month of your own.
  • Minutes 3 to 6. Read the four costs. Ask the room which one they have felt, no names needed.
  • Minutes 6 to 11. Each consultant writes one effort goal for the week (follow-ups, demo drives, call-backs) and one thing that will make them proud of how they served.
  • Minutes 11 to 15. Agree on a daily end-of-day question, "Did I serve well today?" Decide whether the leaderboard stays on the showroom floor, and set the date you will review gross by volume tier in private.

Weekly inspection

Inspect effort and service first. Units and gross are the lagging numbers, reviewed privately.
