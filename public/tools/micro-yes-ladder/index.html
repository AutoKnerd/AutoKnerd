The Micro-Yes Ladder

A one-page ladder for thawing out guarded customers with small, genuine yeses, then bridging those yeses into the big commitment so nobody slams the brakes at the paperwork.

Instant win:On your next customer, open with "Good coffee or dealership coffee?" and keep a tally of every genuine yes through the visit.

Why this matters

Three numbers.

9%of people rank car salespeople's honesty as high
50 to 70%more often, test drives convert compared with visits without one
54%closing rate at the first dealership a buyer visits, when buyers only visit two

Customers arrive with their guard up. Andrew's story: a nervous couple, an anxious husband, drawbridge up. He asked no big questions early. Small, easy yeses stacked up until, by his count, they had nodded about 15 times before the price was ever shown. The yeses had already guided the way to the right car. Another couple nodded at everything, then their faces changed the second he mentioned paperwork, because he never connected the yeses to them owning the car. This page fixes both.

Part 1: The ladder

Low-risk first. Never ask for a rung they might refuse.

First 30 seconds
Goal: an easy, human yes

Say"Can I grab you a bottle of water? Or would you like the good coffee or the dealership coffee?" Expect a little laugh and "you pick." That is all you need.

At the vehicle
Goal: practical, low-pressure agreements

Say"Mind if I pull this one into the shade so we can take a better look?" Then, "Want to see something this model does that most people miss?" Curiosity breeds a yes. If it has self-parking, practice it before you offer it, because those systems usually need a little help.

Before the drive
Goal: approval to move

SayGet approval for the test drive, then keep going on the road: "We'll take a right here, okay?" Offer a feature you know they will enjoy, or their favorite color or trim.

On the numbers
Goal: a yes that helps them, not you

Say"Would it help if I showed you what your monthly payment would look like with that package?" You are educating them that going up costs them a bit more, and that the choice is theirs.

After every yes
Goal: keep momentum alive

SayReinforce it: "Awesome choice." / "That really works well for you." / "Based on what we've talked about, you'll love this."

Genuine yes versus fake yes

Fake authenticity gets fake yeses, and the wall goes back up fast.

Fake yes

"Do you like breathing?" Throwaway questions asked only to stack yeses, or agreement forced through pressure.

It helps no one reach their goal. Pressure also costs you paycheck, so it is not a shortcut.
Genuine yes

A question that moves them toward the car they came for and makes them smile or lean in.

Happier, more connected customers buy, come back and tell friends. A "no" here and there matters little, but do not stack them.

The MICRO check

Andrew's acronym for every yes question.

M: Make the first one easy

Stupidly easy.

I: Integrate naturally

Part of the conversation, never from left field.

C: Confirm positivity

Each question should trigger a positive feeling.

R: Read the intent

Ask with the goal of them leaving in the car, not just collecting yeses.

O: Own the big yes

When a yes makes them smile and lean in, recognize it and build on it.

Part 2: The bridge to the big yes

The yeses only pay off once you connect them to ownership.

1. Anchor the yeses
Goal: make each one real

Say"You told me you love the safety, the space, and the comfort works for you and your family. Did I hear that right?" When they confirm again, the yeses are anchored.

2. Tell their story
Goal: mental ownership

SayDo not just recap. Weave it into a picture of their life: "Picture the road trip. The kids are finally asleep in the back. The safety systems keep you calm in your lane, and you can see the kids are out cold. This car gives everyone comfort."

3. Ask one bigger confirming question
Goal: the right to move to the close

Pick one"Can you see this being the vehicle you trust for the next 5 years?"  /  "It sounds like the car checks all the boxes you told me matter, right?"  /  "On a scale of 1 to 10, how well does this fit your family's needs?" Then: "Is this the one you want to take home today?"

Manager rollout: 15 minutes

Run it in this week's sales meeting.

  • Minutes 0 to 3. Ask the room: when did a customer nod all the way through, then freeze at the paperwork? Read the 9%, 50 to 70% and 54% numbers.
  • Minutes 3 to 6. Walk the ladder and the MICRO check. Everyone writes three yes questions in their own words (word-for-word scripts sound robotic).
  • Minutes 6 to 12. Pairs role-play a guarded customer. The consultant climbs the ladder, then runs the bridge: anchor, story, big confirming question. The partner counts genuine yeses and flags any fake one. Swap.
  • Minutes 12 to 15. Every consultant tallies the genuine yeses on their next customer and notes what put a smile on that customer's face, so it can be repeated with similar personalities.

Weekly inspection

Inspect the yes habit, not the close rate. The close is the lagging number.
