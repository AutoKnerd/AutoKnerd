The What Happens Next Roadmap

Customers do not walk away over price as often as you think. They walk away when they feel lost. A one-page way to tell them what is coming, at every step of the visit.

Instant win: On the very next customer, say out loud what happens next before you walk them to the first step.

The moment a customer starts thinking "what's happening next?", anxiety spikes far higher than it should. Consultants usually think everything is going fine. That disconnect is the problem., you are not always losing deals to objections. Sometimes the customer just does not feel oriented.

What the customer is carrying in

The anxiety is learned, and they bring it through your front door.

The parking lot rehearsal

They sit with hands on the wheel, running scenarios: "Don't say too much. Don't get trapped. Stay sharp." They feel like they have lost before they open the door.

Nobody else does this

You do not brace like this walking into an Apple store or a Best Buy., this is a learned feeling about dealerships.

It is the system, not you

It traces back to a system built over 100 years ago that never updated around how people want to buy. It is not about blaming dealers.

Anxiety has to be managed by someone. The customer is trying to get something done, so that someone is you.

The roadmap

At every handoff, say three things: what we are doing now, what comes next, and how long it takes. Fill in the brackets with your store's real process.

Hello
Goal: they know the plan for the visit

Say"Here's how this usually goes. I'll ask a few questions about what matters to you, we'll look at the right vehicle together, and you decide what happens after that. Sound okay?"

Before the walk-around
Goal: no surprises about where we are going

Say"Next I'm going to show you the vehicle that matches what you told me. Then we'll talk about a drive. Stop me any time."

Before the drive
Goal: they know what the drive is and is not

Say"We'll take a drive of about [time] so you can feel it. After we're back I'll walk you through what comes next."

Before the numbers
Goal: no disappearing, no mystery

Say"I'm going to step away for a few minutes to pull together options for you. Here's what I'm doing and when I'll be back. Can I get you something while you wait?"

Before paperwork and delivery
Goal: they see the finish line

Say"Here's every step left: [list your steps], and about how long each one takes. If anything is unclear, ask me. That's my job."

The one move to change

Replace the silent handoff.

Skip this

"Let me go talk to my manager."

The customer is alone, guessing, and sure the worst is happening.
Say this

"I'm going to check a few things for you. It will take about [time]. When I come back, I'll show you the options and you tell me what you think."

It names the step, the time and the next move. That is orientation.

Manager rollout: 15 minutes

Run it in this week's sales meeting.

  • Minutes 0 to 3. Read the "every three to five years" line. Ask: what is the most confusing moment of our process from the outside?
  • Minutes 3 to 6. As a group, write your store's real steps on the board, from hello to delivery. Mark every spot where the customer is left waiting without being told why.
  • Minutes 6 to 11. Pairs practice the "what happens next" line for each handoff, using the store's own steps and times. The partner plays an anxious customer who asks "what's next?" at each stop.
  • Minutes 11 to 15. Every consultant commits to using the roadmap on every customer today. Pick the day you will listen in on one visit.
